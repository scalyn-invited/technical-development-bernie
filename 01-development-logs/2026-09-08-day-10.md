## Built

Four tables beyond the shipped `users`, plus the `users.role` extension:

- `users` — `role` enum(`administrator`,`member`), indexed, default `member`. No roles table.
- `skills` — unique `name`, nullable `description`, indexed `is_active`, `created_by` FK `nullOnDelete`.
- `development_plans` — `user_id` FK `cascadeOnDelete` and **unique** (one plan per member per cycle), `key_gaps`, `weekly_focus`, `status` enum indexed, `activated_at`/`completed_at`, `created_by` FK `nullOnDelete`.
- `assessments` — plan FK `cascadeOnDelete`, skill FK **`restrictOnDelete`**, `type` enum(`baseline`,`final`), `score` decimal(5,2), `note`, `recorded_by` FK `nullOnDelete`, `recorded_at`. **unique(plan, skill, type)** + index(plan, type).
- `weekly_entries` — plan FK `cascadeOnDelete`, `week_number`, skill FK **`restrictOnDelete`**, `objective`, `evidence`, `outcome_score`, `status` enum, `recorded_by`, `closed_at`. **unique(plan, week_number)** + index(status, week_number).

Five models with every relationship wired, including `DevelopmentPlan belongsToMany Skill` **through `assessments`** with `withPivot('type','score','note','recorded_at')`. Four PHP backed enums in `app/Enums` so no invalid status string can be assigned. Four query scopes (`Skill::active`, `DevelopmentPlan::status`, `Assessment::ofType`, `WeeklyEntry::open`). Factories for all five models with named states (`administrator`, `inactive`, `active`, `completed`, `baseline`, `final`, `evidenced`, `closed`). A seeder producing one administrator, three members at the three plan stages, six active skills and one deactivated skill — in one command.

`TECHNICAL-NOTES.md` started: schema tables, the foreign-key reasoning table, the relationship map, and §5 "what cannot be represented without a migration".

## Learned

The `cascade` / `restrict` / `set null` choice is not three flavours of the same decision — it answers three different questions. `cascade` where the child has no meaning without the parent (a plan without its member). `restrict` where deleting the parent would destroy evidence (a skill carrying recorded scores — this is what turns "deactivate, never delete" from a convention into an enforced rule). `set null` where the parent is provenance rather than a component (who created a skill; who recorded a score). Writing that distinction down as a sentence made the nine foreign keys fall out without further deliberation.

Also: `decimal:2` as an Eloquent cast keeps a score a **string** in PHP rather than a float. For the figure the whole tool exists to report, that is the difference between arithmetic that is exact and arithmetic that is approximately right.

## AI assistance used

- **Block 0–10 (retention, not fresh docs):** as the day card requires, the foreign-key decisions were made from the Day 4 notes on `cascade`/`restrict`/`set null` rather than by re-reading the Laravel documentation. Documentation was consulted only for the `belongsToMany` + `withPivot` signature.
- **Implementation was AI-assisted.** The schema itself — every column, type, nullability, foreign-key behaviour, unique constraint and index — was specified in the Day 10 card before the session started; the AI wrote the migration, model, factory and seeder files to that specification. The enum-class approach in `app/Enums`, the `decimal:2` cast reasoning and the seeder's three-stage dataset were AI suggestions accepted after review.
- **Prompt C (senior review) on the schema:** *"Review this schema against the domain. What will I be unable to represent later without a migration?"* Eight limits returned; all eight are recorded in `TECHNICAL-NOTES.md` §5. The two that changed how I describe the schema are the `decimal(5,2)` range gap and the absence of soft deletes anywhere (deleting a user erases their entire programme record with no recovery path).
- **Validation rule applied:** nothing was accepted as correct on the AI's word. Every claim was proven by running it — `migrate:fresh --seed`, `migrate:rollback --step=5` then `migrate`, every relationship traversed against seeded data, and three constraint tests that had to actually fail (duplicate baseline rejected, assessed skill refusing deletion, member deletion cascading to plan and assessments).

## AI output rejected / corrected

- The AI's first attempt to run the relationship proof used `php artisan tinker --execute="require '...'"`, which **echoed the file source instead of executing it** and produced no evidence at all. Had the output not been read carefully, that could have been filed as a passing verification. Replaced with a script that boots the framework kernel directly and actually runs.
- A `/tmp/...` path was used for the proof script on a Windows machine and silently failed to resolve for PHP. Reminder that "it ran" and "it ran where I think it ran" are different claims.

## Still cannot explain

The constraint demonstrably fired in this run; I cannot yet explain the mechanism that made it fire, or guarantee the same behaviour on the Day 18 deployment target. Open gap carried to Day 11.

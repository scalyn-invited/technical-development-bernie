# Day 11 — Prompt Log — Authentication and Roles/Permissions

## Block 0–10 — retention, not Prompt A

The day card replaces the concept prompt with a retention check: re-read the Day 8 Sanctum and Policy notes, and consult the documentation **only** for what the notes cannot answer, recording what they could not.

**What the Day 8 notes covered adequately:** token issue and revocation, `auth:sanctum` on a route group, the authentication-versus-authorisation distinction, and that a Policy is the single place a permission rule should live.

**What the notes could not answer — documentation consulted:**

1. **How to pass a second argument to a policy method for a `create` action.** Day 8's `OrderPolicy::create(User $user)` took no model, because the decision did not depend on one. Here it does — recording a score depends on *whose plan* it is. Answer: `Gate::authorize('create', [Assessment::class, $plan])` maps to `create(User $user, DevelopmentPlan $plan)`.
2. **Whether Laravel 12 still needs policies registered in an `AuthServiceProvider`.** The Day 8 app predates the question. Answer: policies are auto-discovered by naming convention (`App\Models\X` → `App\Policies\XPolicy`), and no provider registration exists in a Laravel 12 skeleton.

Both were verified by running them, not by accepting the documentation on its own — the matrix output in the evidence log is that verification.

**Output quality:** high, and two findings were genuinely dangerous. Every claim was tested with a real request before being accepted or dismissed.

## AI output that was wrong

1. **`WeeklyEntryPolicy` was first drafted with a softer permission than `AssessmentPolicy`** — the reasoning offered was that logging a week is progress tracking rather than scoring. It is not: a weekly entry carries `outcome_score`. Caught by reading the schema rather than the explanation. Had it shipped, it would have been a route straight around the integrity rule, on an endpoint that does not look like a scoring endpoint.
2. **`DevelopmentPlanPolicy::viewAny` was first drafted to return `false` for members**, which would have turned a member's plan list into a 403. Wrong answer to "show me what I am allowed to see" — the narrowing belongs in the query, not the Policy.
3. **The first rate-limit verification was proposed and run without resetting the limiter**, producing seven consecutive 429s. Correct behaviour, worthless evidence: it never captured the transition from allowed to throttled. A screenshot of it would have looked like proof. This is the second time in two days that AI-proposed *verification* — rather than AI-proposed code — was the thing that was wrong, which is worth noting as a pattern.

## Standing note on this session's method

The rule set was specified in the Day 11 card before the session opened: the four endpoints, the two roles, the read and write permissions, and the integrity rule. The AI wrote the policies, controllers, envelope and limiter to that specification.
